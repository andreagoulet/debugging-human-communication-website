### The Origin

> "I want to tell you how I got here. My **background is in strategic communications**, working with organizations to make their messaging clear so the whole system purrs."

> "In 2009, I went to my **highschool reunion** and my friend Scott asked me to be the CEO of his software company. I had **never coded before**, so I was like "you've got the wrong person."

> "Then he said something I'll never forget. 'Andrea, you might not think you are a programmer, but **you definitely think like a programmer.**'"

> "I'd only ever seen myself as a marketer, but Scott helped me see myself in a new way. So, after some due diligence, **I agreed.** And a few years after running the business together, Scott asked me another question I said yes to, and **we got married.**" 

### The Discovery

> "Now, I had NO idea how **different the software industry was** to the business world I was used to. I constantly felt like a fish out of water. Not only because I was often the only woman in the room, but because **I THOUGHT so differently** than everyone else."

> "On top of that, Scott and I experienced ALL of the typical **salesperson vs. engineering communication challenges** you could think of."

> "I remember one time, there was an urgent client matter, and **I had to get his attention**. For three day's he'd been sitting at his desk with his head between noise cancelling headphones." 

> "I didn't want to bother him, but this was important. So I did a kind of **thought experiment.** What if I sent email? No. Tap him on the shoulder? No. So I decided to sneak up an sheepishly give a little wave — **you got a sec?**"

> "I know that for some of you in the room you've just had a **visceral reaction** to that question. Scott did too. He got so frustrated I thought he was going to **flip the desk** and I was completely taken aback when he sarcastically quipped — 'I do now.'" 

> "A that moment, **I had two choices**. My first instinct was to quip back — **what is WRONG with you?** Because in all my previous jobs, if you needed to get someone's attention, that's what you did. Scott's reaction was completely inappropriate." 

> "**Instead, I paused**. I took a breath. And I decided to focus on the interaction instead of us as individuals."

> "Scott, **I don't know what I did. I took my best guess** at how I could interrupt you without annoying you. I know what you're working on is important, but there's an urgent issue with the client and if we don't address it now, we could lose the account. I put a lot of thought into whether and how to approach you and I can see that **my hypothesis was wrong.** I don't know what I did to make you so upset and I want to learn. There are going to be times that I have to interrupt you. Can we figure out what went wrong here and **figure out a way to communicate better so this doesn't happen again**."

> "Then Scott took a deep breath. "**I'm sorry too.** It's just that when you asked me that question, I was in really deep focus. I've been working on this other client for 3 days to meet a deadline and I was so close to finishing it. I know you might not see it, but there's a lot going on in my brain when I code. I have to build these **complex mental models in my mind** and whene you asked me "do you got a sec?" I had to shift my focus to **figuring out how to answer it**. How long? What's it about? Am I at a good stopping point? That was enough that I started to watch all of the work I'd done to solve the problem crumble — like in that **movie Inception** when the streets of Paris fold in on themselves when the characters realize they're in a dream. I shouldn't have reacted the way I did, and I'm sorry. You're right. We need to figure out how to handle this better in the future." 

> "We went to lunch and **tossed out a bunch of different strategies** — what if we did a Slack notification? We'd probably forget to turn it on and off. Then Scott said, "You know, if you sent me **the word Inception**, I could just reply with a number and that wouldn't take too much effort. If it's zero, that would mean I'm available. If it's high, like a 5, that would mean I'm in deep focus. But I'd know to find you when I'm in a good place."

### The Pattern

> "It worked wonders. All of the sudden, **we'd taken this big problem and figured out this tiny communication tweak** that changed everything for our business, for our marraige, for our sanity. So I shared what we'd learned at conferences and I started getting emails from people who had watched my talks telling me just **how much these ideas had changed their lives and ideas for the better, too.** That's when I decided to dedicate my life to figuring out the fundamentals of what's going on so that more people can solve more problems — just like Scott and I did."

### The Bridge

> "That's why I do this work. **I straddle two worlds — code and communication.** I've done the research and I've discovered that the exact same models are the foundation of both healthy software and healthy social systems. I know how to demystify communication so we can look at its mechanics instead of as magic."

> "You can debug your communication the same way you debug code. And when you do, your technical skills aren't a liablity. They're one of the best assets you have."
