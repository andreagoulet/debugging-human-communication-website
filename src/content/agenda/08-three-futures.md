### Pitch Part 1 — Today and the Future

<p class="stage-direction">Visual: a box labeled "today" on the left, "three years from now" on the right, three branches between them — green (best), yellow (plateau), red (failure). Whiteboard or animated slide.</p>

#### Ask for permission to begin the exercise

> "Before I teach anything, I want to do an exercise with you — and this is the part where we start to dig deeper. So if you don't have something to write with, go ahead and grab a pen or open a document. I'm going to show you how to measure how your communication is impacting your goals and where you want to be so we can see what success looks like. Is it okay if I walk you through it?"

#### Set up the visual

> "Okay. This is where you are today. Over here — three years from now. Between now and then, three things can happen — the best case, the okay case, and the failure case. The gap between them is where we're going to find the leverage for the amplification goal you just named."

#### Best case — GREEN (amplifying)

> "If everything went your way over the next three years — your ideas landed, your work got seen, the right people paid attention — what would be true for you that isn't true today? (**Clarity** = messages land), (**Persuasion** = behavior changes), (**Impact** = influence grows) Type it in the chat. Be specific: a role, a stage, a project, a client, a number."

- You translate complex thinking into words that make people act — the direction you set gets followed, the plan you proposed gets funded, the decision you recommended gets made. No more re-explaining. No more "let's circle back."
- You're closing the clients, deals, and opportunities you used to almost-close — the right message hits the right person the first time.
- You've moved into the role you've been ready for — staff, director, VP, founder, leadership table — because the value of your work is finally visible.
- You're getting invited to speak, publish, or consult on work you actually care about — and saying yes or no on your terms.
- You have a community of other analytical minds who ship work into the world together.
- Your ideas are cited, referenced, built on by others — inside your org and across your field.

#### The plateau — YELLOW (plateaued)

> "Now the okay case. Not disaster — just not transformational. You kept the job. You kept the reputation. Nothing fell apart. But three years pass and nothing actually scaled. What does that version look like for you? Type it in the chat."

<p class="stage-direction">Read 2–3 responses. Acknowledge how easy it is to land here by default — it's not failure, which is exactly what makes it the most common place capable people end up.</p>

- You're respected for your expertise but your public presence has stalled.
- You get local wins but can't replicate them beyond your immediate team.
- You wrote the post, gave the talk, pitched the idea — and it did fine, not great.
- You're not sure why some of your ideas land and others disappear.
- You've read the books, watched the talks — you can't quite apply what you learned to your specific situation.

#### The failure case — RED (stuck below your level)

> "And the failure case. What does it look like three years from now if you can't get the right message to the right people?"

<p class="stage-direction">This prompt is harder to answer — give space. Read 2–3 if they come.</p>

- You have the expertise but your reputation, revenue, and audience don't reflect it.
- You lose deals, opportunities, or budget — not because your thinking is wrong, but because your message didn't land.
- You know you're underselling what you do — clients, investors, or leadership don't understand the full value of what you're offering.
- The ideas that are crystal clear in your mind get jumbled when you try to explain them to someone who thinks differently than you.
- You've stopped proposing things because you can't tell what gets through and what doesn't.

#### Name the cost

> "Look at the space between green and red. That gap represents the difference of having messages that land and make an impact or continuously missing the mark. And this is cumulative, too. Communication compounds. It's not just one conversations, but hundreds, maybe thousands over the course of three years."

<p class="stage-direction">Gesture physically between the green and red branches on the visual. Let the silence land for a beat before moving on.</p>

#### Name the cause — why most people drift into red

> "Here's the thing — nobody plans to end up in the red. Nobody walks into their career thinking, 'I'll have great ideas and watch them never leave my head.' Everybody in this room wants to be up here in the green. But most capable people drift down into the red anyway. Why?"

> "Three reasons. **No framework** — they've never seen communication laid out as a system they can actually reason about. **No community** — they're trying to figure it out alone, without other analytical minds running the same experiments on themselves. **No method** — they have tips and tactics, but no experimental practice for how communication scales. Missing any one of these is enough to keep you stuck. Missing all three is why the drift happens by default."

#### Ask for permission

<p class="stage-direction"><strong>This is the explicit permission ask — the pivot from exercise into offer.</strong> Do not skip it. Do not rush it. Do not treat it as rhetorical. Ask the question, then stop talking. Wait for visible acknowledgments (chat replies, thumbs up, nods on camera) before moving into Pitch Part 2. Asking for permission — and actually receiving it — is what turns this from a pitch into an invited conversation. If the room goes quiet, prompt directly: <em>"Type 'yes' in the chat if you'd like me to walk you through it."</em></p>

> "What I want to do next is share the diagnostic tool I developed to help people move from red or yellow to green. We're going to walk through it systematically so you have time to figure out precisely where your communication gaps are. Is it okay if I walk you through what that looks like?"
