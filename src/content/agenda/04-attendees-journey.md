<p class="stage-direction">Narrate their journey back to them so they feel seen. This is for analytical professionals who want to scale their influence — not for people who feel broken.</p>

#### 1. Transition from your story to theirs

> "So that's my story — but here's what I know about you... **Type in the chat if any of this resonates.**"

#### 2. Reflect who they are

> "**You're good at what you do.** You've put in the years. You've built the systems, solved the hard problems, delivered when it mattered. People come to you when they need a real answer. Your expertise is not in question."

#### 3. The ambition

<blockquote>"And now you want more than expertise. <strong>You want your ideas to turn into action</strong> — in whatever form moving looks like for you.
  <ul>
    <li>Maybe it's getting the <strong>complex idea you've been trying to explain turned into something your organization actually acts on</strong>.</li>
    <li>Maybe it's <strong>closing the deal</strong> on the opportunity you keep almost-landing.</li>
    <li>Maybe it's <strong>moving into the role</strong> you've been ready for — staff engineer, director, VP, founder.</li>
    <li>Maybe it's <strong>speaking at the conferences</strong> you've been attending, <strong>writing the book</strong>, or <strong>growing your business.</strong></li>
    <li>Maybe it's just <strong>being heard at the leadership table</strong> — not because you're the loudest, but because your thinking deserves to be there.</li>
  </ul></blockquote>

#### 4. The friction

<blockquote>"But <strong>there's a gap between how good your work is and the impact it's having.</strong>
  <ul>
    <li>You <strong>create real business value</strong> in the technical details. Maybe you fixed a bug that prevented a huge outage. Or were able to make one tiny tweak to a process that ended up having an outsized impact, but when it comes time for performance reviews, <strong>none of it seems to be noticed.</strong></li>
    <li>You pay attention to the <strong>tiny details of how things will be implemented</strong>, taking the time to guard against every edge case, but then when you go to explain it to your client their <strong>eyes glaze over</strong>. You can tell you're overwhelming them and losing their interest.</li>
    <li>Or you have <strong>ideas that you know are industry changing</strong> and have ambitions of being on stages, writing books, and collaborating with the people you admire, but you just <strong>can't find the words that land</strong>. You over explain. The ideas that are crystal clear in your mind get jumbled when you try to speak or write.</li>
  </ul>
  You know you've got so much potential that is being held back because you haven't figured out the communication patterns that make everything click."</blockquote>

#### 5. Acknowledge their frustration

> "And the frustrating part is — **you KNOW there's a system here.** You can feel it. You can almost see the patterns, the rules, the structure — but every time you try to pin them down, they slip. **You've tried to reverse-engineer** it the way you'd reverse-engineer any other system. And it keeps not quite resolving. Not because you're not smart enough. Because the patterns are real, but the framework to make them visible isn't one you've encountered yet."

#### 6. Acknowledge their efforts

> "You've tried. **You don't sit there and complain.** You've put in the work — reading books, taking courses, watching talks. You've taken away some pretty decent tips and tricks, but **haven't figured out how they all fit together.** When you try to implement it, it doesn't quite click. You can't quite figure out how to adapt it to your specific situation. **You're craving a system, not a collection of tactics.** You want to know exactly what to do and what will work with confidence."

#### 7. Their current desire

> "Right now, **you're ready to work on this the way you'd work on any hard technical problem.** Form hypotheses. Run experiments. Measure results. Iterate. You don't need inspiration. You need a method."

#### 8. The good news

> "And here's the **good news — that's exactly how we're going to work today.** Not with checklists you'll never use. Not with generic frameworks. You're going to leave with a diagnostic of where your communication system has the most leverage — and a clear sense of what to experiment with next."
